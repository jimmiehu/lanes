# Beds, and what grows in them

Four beds, a strip by the shed, and the compost bays at the back. The headings below go four levels deep, which is as deep as Lanes draws: a `####` is still a heading, the smallest of the four. The outline beside the note lists the `##` and `###` ones.

## North bed

Gets the morning sun and nothing after two. Good for anything that bolts.

### This year

Garlic in October, broad beans beside it in November.

#### Garlic

Two varieties, a softneck and a hardneck, forty cloves each. Planted pointed end up, a thumb deep, a hand apart.

#### Broad beans

Sown direct. The first sowing goes under fleece; the second does not, and is usually the one that does better.

### Last year

Potatoes, which left the soil loose and the bed full of volunteers until June.

## South bed

The warm one. Tomatoes against the fence, basil between them, and courgettes at the far end where they can sprawl.

### This year

#### Tomatoes

Six plants, cordon-trained. Side shoots out every Saturday, and the top pinched once there are five trusses.

#### Courgettes

Two plants is one too many. We grew three.

### Notes for next year

Move the tomatoes: blight came in from the plot next door in August, and the south fence holds the damp.

## The long bed

Leeks, then kale over winter. The leeks went in as pencil-thin seedlings dropped into dibbed holes and watered in, never backfilled.

## By the shed

Rhubarb, which was there before us and will be there after. Nobody plants it and nobody moves it.
