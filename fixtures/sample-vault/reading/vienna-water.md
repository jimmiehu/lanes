# Vienna water — historical facts for the river essay

Notes from an afternoon in a library in Vienna, copied out of three books and a museum leaflet. For the essay in [[river-walks]]. Nothing here goes in until it has been checked against a second source — the last section lists what still needs checking.

## Drinking water comes from the mountains, not the river

- The city's drinking water does not come from the Danube. It comes from springs in the Alps, carried to the city by gravity in long aqueducts.
- The first of these mountain spring lines opened in the 1870s; a second followed in the early 1900s.
- The fountain on one of the big squares in the centre was built to mark the first line's opening. It still runs.
- The point for the essay: the river that runs through the city is not the water the city drinks. The river is for floods, transport and looking at.

## The big river was moved

- In the late nineteenth century the Danube was given a new, straight main channel, away from the old centre. The old arms became the canal and a string of lakes.
- The canal walked in walks one, eight and eleven is the arm that was left close to the centre.
- The long island of walk five is much later: dug out of the flood plain in the second half of the twentieth century, as flood protection, and only afterwards a place for a Sunday.

## The small river was put in a channel

- The small river that gives the city its name floods suddenly after rain in the hills to the west. Around 1900 it was put in a stone channel sized for its worst floods.
- The railway beside it was built at the same time and as part of the same works.
- In the centre the river was covered over. That is the stretch where it "goes under" in walks two and ten.
